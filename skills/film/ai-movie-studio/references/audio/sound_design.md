# Sound Design — SFX, Foley & World-Building Through Audio

## Sound Builds the World

Before the first image, a film can be any world. Sound makes it specific. The sound world of the film — its ambient texture, its impact sounds, its Foley layer — determines whether the audience believes in the physical reality of what they see.

Sound design is not adding sound effects to picture. It is building the acoustic architecture of a world.

---

## The Audio Layer Stack

Every scene has these layers. Design each one separately, then mix.

```
Layer 1: DIALOGUE          — The primary story carrier. Always audible and clear.
Layer 2: FOLEY             — The physical sounds of the specific characters: footsteps,
                              clothing, objects they touch.
Layer 3: SFX               — Effects tied to specific on-screen events: weapons, vehicles,
                              impacts, elements.
Layer 4: AMBIENCE          — The continuous sound world of the location.
Layer 5: MUSIC             — Score and source music (music playing in the world).
```

Dialogue always sits on top. Music sits underneath unless the scene is a musical moment.

## Foley Reference

Foley is the reproduction of every sound a character's physical existence makes.

### Footsteps

The most important Foley element. Footsteps tell the audience the weight of the character, the material of the surface, the urgency of their movement.

| Surface | Sound character |
|---|---|
| Stone / concrete | Hard, ringing, resonant |
| Wood floor | Creak and thud — texture varies with floor age |
| Grass / earth | Soft, absorbed, minimal sustain |
| Gravel | Grinding, shifting, complex texture |
| Metal grating | Ring, clang, resonant hollow |
| Carpet | Muffled, soft, near-silent |
| Water / mud | Splash-suck, wet, heavy |
| Sand | Soft crunch, shifting, dry |

**Prompt phrase:** `footsteps on [surface], [weight — light/heavy], [pace — slow/fast], [surface material sound character]`

### Armour and Gear

Armour creates a continuous acoustic signature — metal-on-metal movement with every step.

**Prompt phrase:** `armour sounds with movement, metal pieces shifting and settling with each step, weight audible`

### Cloth and Clothing

High-quality Foley captures the rustle of fabric. In combat, cloth impacts read as physical violence.

**Prompt phrase:** `cloth movement sound, [material — leather/silk/cotton/chain], movement texture`

## Environment Ambience Reference

Every location has a specific sound signature. Name it precisely.

### Interior Environments

| Space | Ambience |
|---|---|
| Cathedral / large stone space | Long reverb, silence inside the silence, distant echoes |
| Cramped underground / cave | Short reverb, drips, compressed acoustic space |
| Office / institutional | Air conditioning hum, distant phones/voices, flat reverb |
| Forest hut / wooden interior | Wood creak, wind outside, warm and enclosed |
| Ship / submarine | Metal hum, engine vibration, creaking hull, pressure |
| Prison cell | Distant institutional sounds, reverb on concrete, isolation |

### Exterior Environments

| Space | Ambience |
|---|---|
| Forest | Bird calls, wind through leaves, insects, distance absorbs sound |
| Desert | Near silence, distant wind, extreme dryness of acoustic |
| Ocean / beach | Continuous wave rhythm, salt air acoustic, birds distant |
| Mountain | Wind dominant, vast space, sound absorbed by distance |
| City street | Traffic, voices, mechanical hum, constant dense texture |
| Battlefield (post-battle) | Silence broken by fire, distant cries, settling debris |
| Ancient ruin | Wind through stone, acoustic resonance of old stone |

**Prompt phrase:** `[location] ambience, [primary elements], [secondary texture], [acoustic character — reverb long/short/dead]`

## Genre SFX Reference

### Gunfire

| Weapon type | Sound character |
|---|---|
| Pistol (small calibre) | Sharp crack, short decay, small echo |
| Pistol (large calibre) | Deeper report, more percussive, physical impact |
| Rifle / assault rifle | Loud report with significant echo, mechanical reload between shots |
| Shotgun | Wide, booming, scattered pellet sound on impact |
| Sniper rifle | High crack, distance-appropriate echo, suppressed environments go silent after |
| Suppressed/silenced | High-pressure burst, mechanical action louder than report |
| Minigun / auto fire | Continuous mechanical rattle, blended fire texture, high rate of fire rhythm |

**Combat audio prompt:** `gunfire, [weapon type], acoustic environment [indoor reverb/outdoor open], distance from camera, [suppressed/unsuppressed], reload sounds`

### Melee / Combat

| Impact type | Sound |
|---|---|
| Fist strike to jaw | Dull thud, body follows, exhale from receiver |
| Body kick | Deeper thud, movement accompaniment |
| Metal weapon clash | Ring and grind, edge contact texture |
| Blade through resistance | Wet, sharp, then silent — the worst sound |
| Bone impact | Hard, dense, no ring |
| Choke / struggle | Gasping, fabric strain, body against floor/wall |

**Prompt phrase:** `combat sound design, [impact types], realistic impact audio, Foley for body movement, [environment reverb]`

### Explosions

| Scale | Sound |
|---|---|
| Grenade | Sharp initial crack, debris scatter, ringing in aftermath |
| Car bomb | Heavy bass thud, glass shattering, car parts moving, fire roar establishing |
| Building demolition | Sustained rumble, structure falling, concrete impact, dust |
| Large military ordnance | Sub-bass felt more than heard, concussion wave, sustained roar |
| Nuclear / catastrophic | Silence at center, building wave, then the sound arrives late |

**Prompt phrase:** `explosion audio, [scale], initial crack, debris scatter, fire establishing, [aftermath — ringing silence / crowd / alarms]`

### Vehicles

| Vehicle | Sound signature |
|---|---|
| Sports car | High-rev engine, tire squeal, speed |
| Military vehicle / tank | Low diesel rumble, track clatter, weight |
| Motorcycle | High-pitched engine, gear changes audible |
| Helicopter | Rotor whop, engine, turbine — low and rhythmic |
| Military jet / fighter | High screech, doppler shift, sonic physicality |
| Large ship | Deep horn, engine vibration through hull, water displacement |

## Sound as Storytelling

The most powerful sound design moments are not effects — they are emotional tools.

### The Silence of Shock

After a large explosion or gunshot at close range, the character's hearing goes out. Replace the full audio world with a high-frequency ring and muffled, underwater versions of all sounds. Then slowly bring it back.

**Prompt phrase:** `audio perspective shift — character's hearing damaged, high-frequency ring, all ambience muffled and underwater, gradual recovery`

### Hearing Only What the Character Hears

In a crowd scene, focus audio on one conversation — everything else becomes indistinct background murmur. The audience's attention is directed by what they can clearly hear.

### Pre-Echo / Sound Before Source

Hearing a sound before its visual source is established — footsteps approaching before the character is seen, a weapon being loaded off-screen. Creates anticipation and dread.

**Prompt phrase:** `[sound] heard before source visible, off-screen audio creating dread/anticipation`

### The Sound That Isn't There

The absence of expected sound creates unease. A battlefield gone silent. A city with no birds. A room where the ambient hum has stopped. The brain registers the missing sound as warning.

**Prompt phrase:** `[normal ambience] absent — silence where sound should be, wrong absence creating unease`


## Golden Rule

> Sound is 50% of the experience but gets 10% of the creative attention. Build the acoustic world first — what does this location sound like at rest? Then add the event sounds on top of that foundation.
