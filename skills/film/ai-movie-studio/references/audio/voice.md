# Voice — Character Voice Design, VO & Lip Sync

## Voice Is Identity

A character's voice is as much their identity as their face. An inconsistent voice across scenes breaks character continuity just as surely as changing the wardrobe between shots.

---

## Character Voice Design

For every major character, define a voice profile. Lock it. Apply it in every prompt where that character speaks.

### Voice Profile Template

```
CHARACTER VOICE LOCK — [Name]:
  Register:      [deep / mid / high]
  Tone quality:  [warm / cold / rough / smooth / raspy / clear]
  Pace:          [deliberate / natural / rapid / variable]
  Accent:        [specific accent — or "neutral American / RP British / etc."]
  Personality vocal expression: [how their character comes through in voice — 
                                  "measures words carefully" / "speaks in bursts" /
                                  "voice drops when genuinely emotional"]
  Emotional tells: [how the voice changes when they're stressed / afraid / angry / hiding something]
```

## Voice by Character Archetype

### The Authority Figure

Deep, measured, unhurried. Every word placed with purpose. Does not raise volume when challenged — lowers it.

**Prompt phrase:** `deep measured voice, deliberate pace, does not raise volume under pressure, every word weighted`

### The Threat / Antagonist

Often quiet. The danger is in the restraint. Volume control is the tell.

**Prompt phrase:** `quiet and controlled, menace in the restraint, voice that doesn't need to rise to dominate the room`

### The Young/Scared Character

Higher register, pace irregular — rushing and stopping. Breath audible. Voice breaks at key moments.

**Prompt phrase:** `voice slightly elevated in register, irregular pace under stress, breath audible, controlled breaking at emotional peaks`

### The Soldier / Warrior

Economical. Words spent like ammunition. Not warm, not cold — efficient.

**Prompt phrase:** `military cadence, economical word choice, flat affect except under direct emotion, no wasted syllables`

### The Intellectual

Precise word choice. Slight pause before speaking — words chosen carefully. No filler. Academic register.

**Prompt phrase:** `precise vocabulary, slight deliberative pause before complex statements, measured academic delivery`

### The Traumatized

Flat. Dissociated. Speaking about terrible things as if they happened to someone else.

**Prompt phrase:** `flat affect, speaking from distance, emotional content of words does not match delivery register, dissociative cadence`

## Narrator Voice Design

Voice Over narration has a different relationship to the scene than dialogue — it is the film speaking directly to the audience.

### Omniscient Narrator

Knows everything, speaks from outside time.

**Voice quality:** Deep, resonant, measured, authority without judgment.

**Prompt phrase:** `narrator, deep resonant voice, omniscient tone, outside of time, speaks with measured authority`

### Unreliable Narrator (Character Voice)

The story is told from a character's perspective — which may be incomplete, biased, or false.

**Voice quality:** More personal, more particular, hints of emotion or agenda beneath.

**Prompt phrase:** `first-person narrator, character voice, subjective — speaks as if certain, [emotional undercurrent]`

### Documentary Narrator

Authoritative, factual, neutral — or narrating events with deliberate emotional detachment.

**Prompt phrase:** `documentary narrator, authoritative and neutral, factual delivery, occasional gravity at significant moments`

## Breathing Design

Breathing is performance. In high-quality audio direction, breathing is not background — it is information.

| Breathing pattern | What it communicates |
|---|---|
| Slow, full, controlled | Calm, confidence, controlled danger |
| Shallow, rapid | Fear, running, shock |
| Held then released | Something processed, decision made |
| Audible effort on exhale | Physical exhaustion, pain |
| Near-inaudible, minimal | Tension, hiding, controlled suppression |
| Ragged, uneven | Grief, extreme distress |

**Prompt phrase:** `breathing [pattern], audible in the mix, [what it communicates]`

## Vocal Extremes

### Battle Shout / War Cry

Guttural, from the body, volume past control — the sound of a person past the point of calculation.

**Prompt phrase:** `battle shout, guttural from the chest, total volume, no vocal control — pure physical aggression`

### Whisper

Can carry more threat than volume. The intimacy of a whisper makes it invasive.

**Prompt phrase:** `whisper, close-mic intimate, [emotional register — threatening / comforting / confessing], breath audible`

### Scream

Varies entirely by context. A scream of grief sounds nothing like a scream of terror.

**Prompt phrase:** `[grief scream / terror scream / rage shout], [context], [duration — single burst / sustained]`

### Laugh

Authentic laughter is physically involuntary. Direct its source.

**Prompt phrase:** `authentic laugh, [what causes it — surprise / dark humor / relief / joy], [duration and decay]`

### Sob / Cry

**Prompt phrase:** `crying, [type — silent tears with controlled voice / audible sobbing / breakdown], [suppressed or released]`

## Spatial Voice Design

How a voice occupies space in the mix depends on physical location in the scene.

| Position | Mix treatment |
|---|---|
| Close-up speaker | Dry, intimate, minimal reverb, close-mic feel |
| Medium distance | Light room ambience, natural environment |
| Shouting across space | Light distortion of distance, acoustic environment present |
| Off-screen voice | Distance and environment without visual reference |
| Radio / phone | Bandwidth-limited, slight compression, device colour |
| Crowd / interior | Voice competes with ambient, less clear separation |

## Lip Sync Technical Reference

Lip sync works when:
- Shot is a held close-up or medium close-up
- No cut occurs during the spoken line
- The mouth is clearly visible and not obscured
- The line duration matches the shot duration
- Only one character is speaking

Lip sync fails when:
- Shot contains motion blur (moving camera)
- The face is at an angle or partially turned
- Multiple characters are in the same tight frame
- The line is longer than the shot

**Safe lip sync prompt:** `held medium close-up, subject faces camera, mouth fully visible, no camera movement, single speaker — lip sync to: "[exact line]"`

---

## Golden Rule

> Voice is the character from the inside. Design it as specifically as a face: register, pace, texture, how it changes under pressure. Lock it. Apply it consistently.
