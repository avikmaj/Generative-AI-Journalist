# Mixing — Layering, Balance & Spatial Audio

## The Mix Is a Hierarchy

Every element in a film's audio has a position in a hierarchy. The mix enforces that hierarchy. Nothing competes with dialogue for the audience's primary attention. Everything else supports the story from its assigned position.

---

## The Mix Hierarchy

```
Level 1: DIALOGUE          — Always intelligible. Nothing fights it.
Level 2: KEY SFX           — Critical story sounds (gunshot, door slam, specific impact)
Level 3: FOLEY             — Physical world texture (footsteps, cloth, objects)
Level 4: MUSIC             — Emotional support layer
Level 5: AMBIENCE          — Continuous world texture (lowest level, constant)
```

Exception: In a music-dominant moment (montage, theme statement, non-dialogue scene), the score rises to Level 1 and ambience/Foley support underneath.

---

## Spatial Audio Concepts

### Stereo Field

Left, center, right positioning in a standard stereo mix.

| Element | Default position |
|---|---|
| Dialogue | Center |
| Score | Wide stereo (L/R) |
| Ambient left sources | Slightly left |
| Ambient right sources | Slightly right |
| On-screen SFX | Panned to screen position of source |
| Off-screen SFX | Panned to suggest direction of source |

### Surround (5.1 / 7.1)

- **Front L/C/R:** Dialogue, primary SFX, music
- **Surrounds:** Ambience, passing effects, score width, crowd
- **LFE (subwoofer):** Explosions, vehicles, bass impacts, physical weight of events

### Dolby Atmos (Object-Based)

Individual sounds placed as objects in three-dimensional space. A helicopter passes overhead — it actually moves from front to rear ceiling to rear. Rain falls from above. An aircraft races directly overhead.

**Prompt phrase:** `Dolby Atmos spatial placement — [source] positioned overhead/behind/passing, immersive three-dimensional placement`

## Dynamics Management

### Dialogue Dynamics

Dialogue must be consistent in level even when performance dynamics vary. A whisper must still be audible; a shout must not distort.

**Design:** Keep dialogue in a 6–8dB dynamic range in the mix. Use compression and automation.

### Music Dynamics in Dialogue Scenes

Music during dialogue scenes sits 8–15dB below dialogue. When dialogue stops, music can rise. This natural breathing creates the "music filling the emotional spaces" effect.

**Prompt phrase:** `music underneath dialogue at [low/distant] level, rises when dialogue stops, breathes with the scene`

### Impact Dynamics

A major impact (explosion, gunshot, punch) is effective when what comes before it is relatively quiet. Constant maximum volume produces fatigue. Design the quiet that precedes the impact.

## Mixing for Scene Type

### Tense / Quiet Dialogue Scene

- Dialogue: clean, close, slight room tone
- Music: none or near-inaudible low pulse
- Ambience: minimal — the room's silence is part of the scene
- SFX: only what is story-relevant (a clock, a distant siren — placed deliberately)

**Prompt phrase:** `quiet tense mix, clean dialogue dominant, near-silence ambient, minimal music — absence of sound is the tension`

### Action / Combat Scene

- SFX dominant alongside dialogue when present
- Music hard in (percussive, driving)
- Foley at full level (footsteps, impacts, gear)
- Ambience buried under SFX
- Dynamics: wide range — maximum impact at key moments, slightly lower between

**Prompt phrase:** `action mix, SFX and music both pushing, full Foley, dialogue cut through at key moments, wide dynamics`

### Emotional / Climactic Scene

- Dialogue: prominent but possibly quieter delivery
- Music: rises to primary position at the right moment
- Ambient: softened — the world falls away as emotion peaks
- SFX: minimal — only what the story requires

**Prompt phrase:** `emotional mix, score rises to foreground, ambient world retreats, music and dialogue share the space`

### Horror Scene

- Dialogue: close, intimate, vulnerable
- Ambience: present and wrong — something slightly off about the ambient world
- SFX: sparse but high-impact, silence between
- Music: sub-bass textures, absence of melody that offers comfort

**Prompt phrase:** `horror mix, ambient world slightly wrong, SFX minimal and precise, musical texture under rather than over, silence weaponized`

## Mix Checklist

```
[ ] Dialogue always intelligible at all levels
[ ] No element fights dialogue except at intentional musical moments
[ ] SFX panned to match screen position of source
[ ] Ambience establishes location at consistent underlying level
[ ] Music level appropriate to scene type
[ ] Impact moments have quiet that precedes them
[ ] Spatial audio consistent with camera position and scene geography
[ ] Transitions between scenes have appropriate audio handling
```

---

## Golden Rule

> Mix toward clarity, not density. The audience should feel the audio world is full without being aware of how many layers are present. The mix disappears; the film remains.
