# Cinematography — Shot Types & Emotional Context

## The Shot Is a Statement

Every shot tells the audience how to feel about what they're seeing. Shot size is not a neutral technical decision — it is the director's opinion about emotional proximity.

---

## Shot Size Reference

### Extreme Wide Shot (EWS)

**What it shows:** Character is tiny in a vast environment. The world dominates.

**Emotional impact:** Isolation, insignificance, scale, freedom, dread of the unknown, epic grandeur.

**When to use:** Establishing a new world. Showing a character at their lowest point, dwarfed by forces beyond them. Opening frames of an epic. A protagonist stepping into a world they cannot control.

**Prompt phrase:** `extreme wide shot, character tiny in foreground, environment fills the frame, vast scale`

---

### Wide Shot (WS)

**What it shows:** Full character in context. We see them and where they are.

**Emotional impact:** Geography, placement, relationship to environment, entrance or arrival.

**When to use:** Introducing a location alongside a character. Showing characters in relation to each other within a space. Physical action with spatial context.

**Prompt phrase:** `wide shot, full figure visible, environment prominent, character in context`

---

### Full Shot (FS)

**What it shows:** Entire body, head to toe.

**Emotional impact:** Physicality, costume, posture, physical state. The whole person.

**When to use:** Introducing a character's visual design. Action that reads in full-body. Power displays.

**Prompt phrase:** `full shot, head to toe, full body visible, ground at frame bottom`

---

### Medium Wide (MW)

**What it shows:** Character from thighs or knees up. Body language visible, face readable.

**Emotional impact:** Balanced — environment and person share the frame.

**When to use:** Two-person conversations where the relationship to space matters. Action where hand gestures and posture are story-relevant.

**Prompt phrase:** `medium wide shot, subject from knees up, body language and expression visible`

---

### Medium Shot (MS)

**What it shows:** Waist to head. The workhorse shot.

**Emotional impact:** Neutral engagement. Close enough to read expression, wide enough to show gestures.

**When to use:** Dialogue scenes. Character in action that reads from the torso up. Balanced coverage.

**Prompt phrase:** `medium shot, waist to head, natural conversation framing`

---

### Medium Close-Up (MCU)

**What it shows:** Chest to head. Face reads clearly, shoulders and posture visible.

**Emotional impact:** Engagement, intimacy beginning. We're starting to focus on the person, not the world.

**When to use:** Dialogue intensifying. Reaction shots. Character processing information.

**Prompt phrase:** `medium close-up, chest to head, face prominent, expression legible`

---

### Close-Up (CU)

**What it shows:** Face fills the frame. Eyes are the scene.

**Emotional impact:** Maximum intimacy, emotional revelation, psychological exposure. Nothing exists but this person and this moment.

**When to use:** The most important emotional moment in a scene. A decision being made. A reaction to devastating news. A lie being told. A truth finally admitted.

**Prompt phrase:** `close-up, face fills frame, shallow focus, eyes sharp, everything else falls away`

---

### Extreme Close-Up (ECU)

**What it shows:** Eyes, mouth, a hand, an object detail. Fragment of a person or thing.

**Emotional impact:** Obsession, intensity, violence implied, detail elevated to significance. Uncomfortable proximity.

**When to use:** A specific detail that holds the story's key. A weapon. A scar. Trembling hands. Eyes that reveal everything in the moment before action.

**Prompt phrase:** `extreme close-up, [specific detail] fills frame, ultra-shallow depth of field, hyper-detail`

---

### Over-the-Shoulder Shot (OTS)

**What it shows:** One character, seen from behind the shoulder of another.

**Emotional impact:** Two-sided tension. Power dynamic implicit. We're in the conversation, not watching it.

**When to use:** Dialogue scenes. Interrogation. Confrontation. Interview. Any scene where the relationship between two people is the subject.

**Prompt phrase:** `over-the-shoulder shot, foreground shoulder and head, subject facing camera in soft focus beyond, conversation framing`

---

### Point of View (POV)

**What it shows:** What the character sees. The camera is the character's eyes.

**Emotional impact:** Total identification. We become the character. Danger is our danger.

**When to use:** First-person danger. Discovery through a character's eyes. A moment of horror, wonder, or recognition where identification is the goal.

**Prompt phrase:** `POV shot, first-person perspective, handheld organic movement, as if seen through character's eyes`

---

### Insert Shot

**What it shows:** A specific object or detail, cut away from the main action.

**Emotional impact:** Significance signaled. The director says: this matters.

**When to use:** A clue. A weapon. A photograph. A reaction object. Anything the audience must notice for story reasons.

**Prompt phrase:** `insert shot, [object] isolated, clean background, deliberately framed to signal importance`

---

### Two-Shot

**What it shows:** Two characters in the same frame.

**Emotional impact:** Relationship, power balance, spatial dynamic between characters.

**When to use:** Establishing or reinforcing the relationship between two characters. The moment of agreement or disagreement.

**Prompt phrase:** `two-shot, both characters visible, relationship framing, [power dynamic note — equal / dominant-subordinate]`

---

## Specialty Shots

### Drone Shot

**Purpose:** Scale, geography, surveillance, god's-eye perspective.

**Emotional impact:** Vastness, isolation, the indifference of the world to human drama, power and overview.

**Best use:** Opening a film. Revealing an army. Tracking a vehicle through a world. The world seen without a human's limitation.

**Prompt:** `aerial drone shot, looking down / tracking forward, vast environment, subjects below as small figures`

---

### Crane / Jib Shot

**Purpose:** Vertical revelation. Rising above or descending into a scene.

**Emotional impact:** Hope, triumph (rise), revelation, doom (descent), scale of environment.

**Best use:** A hero moment — literally rising. Revealing the true scope of a battlefield. The climactic reveal of the goal.

**Prompt:** `crane shot, camera rises from [start height] to [end height], reveal composition, fluid vertical motion`

---

### Handheld

**Purpose:** Immediacy, instability, documentary truth.

**Emotional impact:** Chaos, urgency, danger, psychological instability, reality unravelling.

**Best use:** Combat. Pursuit. Panic. Mental fracture. Scenes where the world is out of control.

**Prompt:** `handheld camera, organic movement, slight instability, urgency, authentic immediacy`

---

### Steadicam / Gimbal

**Purpose:** Fluid movement without the instability of handheld. Can follow action in a way a dolly cannot.

**Emotional impact:** Confident, immersive flow. The camera glides with purpose through space.

**Best use:** Following a character through a complex environment. Corridor scenes. The long take that builds tension.

**Prompt:** `steadicam tracking shot, fluid movement, follows subject through environment, no camera shake`

---

### Dutch Angle (Canted Frame)

**Purpose:** The frame is deliberately tilted off the horizontal axis.

**Emotional impact:** Psychological disturbance, wrongness, danger, instability of perception.

**Best use:** Villain's POV. A moment of mental fracture. Scenes where the world is deeply wrong.

**Prompt:** `dutch angle, frame tilted [X] degrees, sense of unease, psychological discomfort`

---

### IMAX Frame

**Purpose:** Maximum image information, enormous scale.

**Emotional impact:** The most overwhelming version of any shot. Scale becomes overwhelming.

**Best use:** The spectacular natural world. The full scope of a battle. The reveal of an impossible structure.

**Prompt:** `IMAX framing, full aspect ratio, extreme detail, epic scale, every surface visible`

---

### Anamorphic Lens

**Purpose:** Wide aspect ratio, characteristic lens flares, horizontal bokeh.

**Emotional impact:** Cinematic, romantic, epic. The most "filmic" look.

**Best use:** Big productions that want the classic Hollywood cinematic aesthetic.

**Prompt:** `anamorphic lens, 2.39:1 aspect ratio, horizontal lens flares, oval bokeh, cinematic widescreen`

---

## Shot Purpose Quick Reference

| Dramatic Need | Recommended Shot |
|---|---|
| Introduce the world | EWS or WS |
| Introduce a character | FS or MS |
| Establish geography between characters | WS or Two-shot |
| Reveal an important emotion | CU or MCU |
| Show a critical detail | ECU or Insert |
| Create identification with danger | POV |
| Show psychological instability | Dutch Angle or ECU |
| Climactic reveal | Crane rise or Drone |
| Combat / chaos | Handheld WS or MS |
| Intimate dialogue | OTS alternating, or MCU |
| Epic scale | EWS, Drone, Crane |
| Power display | Low angle MS or FS |
| Vulnerability | High angle CU or EWS |

## Lens Reference

| Focal Length | Character | Emotional Use |
|---|---|---|
| 14–18mm | Ultra wide, distortion at edges | Scale, grandeur, unease |
| 24mm | Wide, slight distortion | Environmental epics, action, scale |
| 35mm | Slightly wide, feels natural | Observational, documentary, character in world |
| 50mm | Normal, closest to human eye | Honest, neutral, drama |
| 85mm | Short telephoto, face-flattening | Intimacy, romance, revealing |
| 105–135mm | Telephoto, compression | Surveillance, voyeurism, isolation of subject |
| 200mm+ | Long telephoto, heavy compression | Distance, threat, the world pressing in |

---

## Golden Rule

> The shot size tells the audience how much to care about what's inside it. Choose every frame as a deliberate statement about emotional priority.
