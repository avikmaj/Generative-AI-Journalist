# Screenplay — Format, Scene Writing & Dialogue

## Industry Standard Format

A screenplay is a production document, not a narrative. Every formatting convention exists to give the crew precise information efficiently.

### Master Format Rules

- Font: Courier 12pt (industry standard — 1 page ≈ 1 minute of screen time)
- Margins: 1.5" left, 1" right, top, bottom
- Scene headings: ALL CAPS
- Character names above dialogue: ALL CAPS, centered
- Action lines: present tense, active voice
- Parentheticals: used sparingly, only when delivery is non-obvious

---

## Scene Heading (Slugline)

```
INT. LOCATION — DAY/NIGHT
EXT. LOCATION — DAY/NIGHT
INT./EXT. VEHICLE — MOVING — DAY
```

Examples:
```
INT. POLICE INTERROGATION ROOM — NIGHT
EXT. BATTLEFIELD — KURSK — DAY — 1943
INT. ABANDONED FACTORY — CONTINUOUS
```

---

## Action Lines

Written in present tense. Describe only what the camera sees and what sound can capture. Never describe internal states directly — show them through behavior.

**Rules:**
- Short paragraphs. Maximum 4 lines per block. White space breathes.
- Active voice. "He runs." Not "He is running."
- Only what the audience can see or hear. Not "She is terrified inside."
- Camera directions in action lines only when they are the story. Not "CLOSE ON her face." But: "The camera finds her through the smoke."

**Good action writing:**
```
Rain. A body face-down in the gutter.

DETECTIVE RAINES (50s, hollowed out, good suit gone bad) crouches over it.
He doesn't flinch. He's seen too many.

He reaches into the victim's jacket. Pulls out a single playing card — the 
Ace of Spades. Studies it.

His jaw tightens.
```

## Dialogue Format

```
                    CHARACTER NAME
          (parenthetical — only if needed)
     Dialogue line goes here, indented and
     centered under the name.
```

**Parenthetical use — sparingly:**
```
                    VASQUEZ
          (into radio)
     Command, we have a problem.
     
                    CHEN
          (the thing she's been afraid to say)
     He's not coming back.
```

## Director's Notes in Script

Embed production notes for each scene using these labeled sections. They do not appear on screen — they brief the virtual crew.

```
[DIRECTOR NOTE: The scene should feel like a pressure cooker. 
Tight framing, minimal movement. The room closes in.]

[DP NOTE: Low key. Single practical — a bare bulb. Heavy shadows. 
No fill. 2700K warmth gone cold at contrast.]

[SOUND NOTE: Distant air conditioning hum throughout. 
No music. Silence is the antagonist.]

[VFX NOTE: Subtle heat shimmer from the lamp. Nothing more.]

[EDITOR NOTE: Hold the two-shot longer than comfortable. 
Cut only when the silence has become unbearable.]
```

## Scene Description Templates

### Action / Combat Scene

```
INT./EXT. LOCATION — TIME

[DIRECTOR NOTE: Choreographed chaos — the audience should feel disoriented 
alongside the protagonist. One clear through-line amid the noise.]

[DP NOTE: Handheld. Shallow focus. Cut fast but not random — 
every cut has a clear action/reaction anchor.]

The world has become noise and debris.

PROTAGONIST fights through — not heroically, but desperately.
Every move is survival, not style.

A SOLDIER rounds the corner. Point blank.

Protagonist reacts — faster than thought. Pure instinct.

The soldier drops.

Protagonist stares at what they've done. Half a second.
Then the noise returns and they run.
```

### Intimate / Emotional Scene

```
INT. KITCHEN — DAWN

[DIRECTOR NOTE: Everything unspoken. The dialogue is almost beside the point. 
Watch the hands, the eyes, the space between them.]

[DP NOTE: Static. 50mm. Warm available light — the window behind. 
Don't cut unless the scene demands it.]

MARA sits at the table. Cup in both hands. Steam rising.

Her husband DAVID enters. Stops when he sees her.

Long silence. He doesn't sit.

                    DAVID
     You know.

She doesn't answer. Doesn't look up.

He sits anyway. Across from her — the full table between them.

                    MARA
     How long?

He opens his mouth. Closes it.

The steam from her cup drifts between them.
```

### Horror / Tension Scene

```
INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT

[DIRECTOR NOTE: The audience should be ahead of the character 
but unable to warn them. Build dread, not shock.]

[DP NOTE: Available light only — a failing flashlight. 
Shadow and negative space do the work. Never show everything.]

[SOUND NOTE: Silence broken by irregularities — a drip, 
a settling, something that might be breathing.]

CLAIRE moves the light across the walls.

Boxes. Pipes. The furnace.

She exhales — relief she didn't know she needed.

She turns to leave.

The light catches something.

A hand.

Under the furnace.

Perfectly still.

She doesn't scream. That's what she tells herself later — that 
she didn't scream.

She lied.
```

## Full Scene Structure Template

```
[SCENE NUMBER]. INT./EXT. LOCATION — TIME

[DIRECTOR NOTE: dramatic purpose, tone, emotional target]
[DP NOTE: visual approach, lens, lighting philosophy]
[SOUND NOTE: audio world for this scene]

[ESTABLISHING action — 2-3 lines max, set world and mood]

[CHARACTER INTRODUCTION — first time we see them, describe once, never again]

[SCENE ACTION — present tense, beats of conflict/obstacle/decision]

[DIALOGUE — when scene earns it, not before]

[EXIT BEAT — the last image/moment, designed deliberately]
```


## Dialogue Craft Reference

| Technique | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Deflection | Character answers a different question | "Where were you?" / "The coffee's getting cold." |
| Subtext reversal | Line means the opposite of its surface | "You've done enough." (meaning: get away from me) |
| Interruption | Power and urgency expressed by cutting off | "The plan is to—" / "I don't care about the plan." |
| Silence extension | What isn't said is the real statement | [Long pause after a question. Character looks away. No answer.] |
| Echo | A line that repeats to mean something different | "It's fine." (Act I: reassurance) / "It's fine." (Act III: surrender) |
| False agreement | Character says yes, means no | "Whatever you think is best." [Said while staring at the floor] |
| Displacement | Emotion expressed at wrong target | "This parking spot is ridiculous." [The character is actually devastated about something else] |

## Scene Checklist

Before locking any scene for production:

```
[ ] Scene has a clear dramatic purpose in the larger story
[ ] Something changes — status quo is different at end than start
[ ] Character has a specific goal in this scene
[ ] There is a specific obstacle
[ ] Subtext is present — what isn't said?
[ ] Action lines are present tense, visual, specific
[ ] Dialogue earns its place — every line does work
[ ] Exit beat is designed deliberately
[ ] Director / DP / Sound notes written
[ ] Generation brief translated from scene
```

---

## Golden Rule

> Write only what the camera can capture and the microphone can record. The rest lives in performance, not words.
