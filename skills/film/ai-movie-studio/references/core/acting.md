# Acting — Performance, Physicality & Emotional Truth

## Performance Is the Film

You can have perfect cinematography and perfect lighting and a bad performance will ruin the scene. AI-generated performance lives or dies on how precisely the prompt communicates the internal state.

---

## The Method Approach for AI Direction

Instead of directing surface behavior, direct from the inside out:

**Level 1 (surface):** "She looks angry." → Model renders generic anger.
**Level 2 (state):** "She is trying not to show she is angry." → Model renders control with leak.
**Level 3 (full):** "She has been holding this anger for three years. This is the moment she can't anymore. But she has learned that showing it gives him power, so she is clamping down on it with everything she has — and failing." → Model renders a layered, specific, human performance.

The depth of the performance prompt determines the depth of the result.

## Body Language Reference

### Power High (Confidence, Dominance)

- Spine straight, head level or slightly back
- Shoulders back and relaxed
- Legs shoulder-width or wider
- Weight evenly distributed
- Arms either loose at sides or deliberately placed
- Movement economical — no unnecessary motion
- Eye contact sustained, not urgent

**Prompt phrase:** `confident posture, spine upright, shoulders back, unhurried movement, owns the space`

### Power Low (Fear, Submission, Defeat)

- Spine curved inward
- Shoulders forward and up (protective of throat and chest)
- Arms crossed or held close to body
- Weight shifted back
- Head down or looking up through lowered brow
- Movement either frozen or overly urgent

**Prompt phrase:** `collapsed posture, shoulders forward, contracted body language, weight back, defensive physical stance`

### Contained Threat (Predatory, Controlled Danger)

- Completely still
- Economy of movement — when they move, it means something
- Eye contact that doesn't break
- Relaxed but unhurried
- Movement slow, deliberate, space-owning

**Prompt phrase:** `predatory stillness, minimal movement, absolute eye contact held, controlled and dangerous energy`

## Walking Cycles

Walking style reveals character instantly.

| Walk Type | Character It Signals |
|---|---|
| Long, purposeful stride, eyes forward | Determination, mission, power |
| Short steps, weight forward, pace fast | Anxiety, urgency, stress |
| Deliberate slow stride, head level | Authority, control, patience |
| Shuffling, weight low | Defeat, exhaustion, age, hopelessness |
| Loose, rhythmic, slightly bounced | Confidence, youth, energy |
| Rigid, mechanical | Military, controlled, repressed |
| Tilted lean-forward | Aggression, approach, threat |

**Prompt phrase:** `[character] walks with [type] — [add: toward/away from what, through what environment]`

---

## Running

Running in crisis or combat reads differently from running in joy.

| Type | Physicality |
|---|---|
| Fear-running | Arms high, upper body tilted forward, eyes white, full sprint |
| Combat running | Lower center of gravity, arms close to body, controlled, direction changing |
| Athletic running | Efficient, economical, pace-adjusted |
| Exhausted running | Heavy footfalls, staggered, breath visible, barely maintained |
| Desperate running | Everything given, form deteriorating, survival only |

**Prompt phrase:** `desperate sprint, upper body pitched forward, arms driving, breathing visible, nothing held back`

## Combat and Fight Choreography

**Direct combat from intent, not technique.**

**Bad:** "He punches the other man."
**Good:** "He has nothing left but rage. The blow is not controlled — it is eight years of humiliation launching itself. It connects badly, shoulder not behind it, but the intention is everything."

### Combat Styles — Reference

| Style | Physical character |
|---|---|
| Military CQC | Economical, minimal, lethal efficiency — no wasted motion |
| Street fighting | Improvised, brutal, objects-as-weapons, survival |
| Martial arts | Structured, technique visible, clean angles and counters |
| Brawl | Chaotic, clinching, wrestling mixed with strikes, exhausting |
| Knife fight | Close quarters, defensive movement dominant, fear present |
| Gunfight | Cover-driven, movement tactical, sound dominant |
| Sword / melee | Full body engagement, distance management, footwork critical |

### Combat Cinematography Notes

From `cinematography.md`:
- Close-range combat: handheld medium, cutting fast on action
- Choreographed/stylized combat: wider shots to show full body, stable camera
- Desperate or chaotic combat: handheld, slightly undercranked, tight and disorienting
- Aftermath of combat: wide, pull back, static — let the devastation be seen

## Emotional Transitions

Characters don't snap between emotions — they transition. Design the transition.

```
Shock → Grief:
First seconds: total stillness, face blank (shock processing)
Then: something physical gives way — a shoulder drops, a hand moves
Then: the breath that was held releases, changes character
Then: the face opens, the control dissolves
Finally: whatever grief looks like for this specific character

Calm → Rage:
First: stillness, a tell — hand tightens, jaw sets
Then: control applied actively — stillness becomes effortful
Then: the thing that breaks the control
Then: release — contained or explosive depending on character
```

**Prompt phrase:** `[Character] processes [event] — begins controlled, then [specific physical sequence as emotion builds/breaks through]`

## Micro-Expressions

Micro-expressions flash across the face in a fraction of a second. In cinema, they reveal what the character is suppressing. In a CU, they are the scene.

| What's suppressed | Micro-expression |
|---|---|
| Fear (masked as calm) | Momentary widening of eyes, half-second before control returns |
| Contempt (masked as neutrality) | Single corner of mouth twitches upward then returns |
| Love (masked as professionalism) | Eyes soften for half a second, focus drifts then snaps back |
| Guilt (masked as innocence) | Gaze drops fractionally, then compensatory direct look |
| Triumph (masked as humility) | A breath that comes in too full, suppressed |

**Prompt phrase:** `CU on face — a [micro-expression] crosses his features for less than a second before the mask returns`

## Crowd Performance Direction

Background performance texture:
- In a tense scene, background performers move more slowly, speak less, occupy less space
- In an overwhelming scene, they fill the frame with activity the foreground characters cannot fully track
- In a lonely scene, they are present but create no eye contact, no connection
- In a celebratory scene, their energy should threaten to pull focus — but not quite

## Voice and Dialogue Delivery

Direction for vocal performance:

| Delivery Type | When to Use |
|---|---|
| Flat, measured cadence | Suppressed emotion, control, threat below the surface |
| Rising energy | Escalating urgency, fear, excitement building |
| Dropping volume | A truth arriving — intimacy, confession, the thing finally said |
| Broken sentence | Overwhelming emotion prevents completion |
| Too even | Deliberate calm that signals dangerous control |
| Overlapping | Characters more interested in their own point than the other's |
| Long pause before speaking | Processing, decision being made, weight |

**Prompt phrase:** `[Character] speaks [cadence] — [what the voice is doing beneath the words]`

---

## Golden Rule

> Tell the model what the character is experiencing. The body and face will follow. Generic emotions produce generic performances. Specific human states produce specific human performances.
