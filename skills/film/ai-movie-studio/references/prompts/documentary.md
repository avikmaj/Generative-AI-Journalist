# Documentary Prompts — Observational & Factual Cinema

## Documentary Truth Is Constructed

The most powerful documentary moments are not spontaneous — they are composed. The choice of where to point the camera, what to include and exclude, which moment to hold on — these are directorial choices as deliberate as any fiction film.

---

## Documentary Styles

### Observational / Direct Cinema

Camera watches without interfering. No narration, no interviews, no music that didn't already exist in the scene.

```
STYLE: observational documentary
Camera: handheld, follows action without anticipating it
Sound: environment only — no score, dialogue captured as it happens
Lighting: available light only — never manipulated
Editing feel: long takes, real time, no false acceleration
Visual quality: verite — imperfect, human, immediate
```

### Expository / Narrator-Led

The traditional documentary — narrator explains, images illustrate.

```
STYLE: expository documentary
Narrator: [voice type — authoritative / intimate / character-specific]
Visual: images that support or extend what narrator says — never just literal illustration
Music: [score present — supportive, emotional but not overwhelming]
Talking heads: [interview subjects — who, framed how]
```

### Essay / Cinematic Documentary

Personal, poetic, filmmaker's voice present.

```
STYLE: cinematic essay documentary
Visual approach: [composed, lit, cinematically intentional]
Narrator: [first person / philosophical / questioning]
Music: [integral — score is part of the argument]
Image: [may use re-enactment / archival / original footage / mixed]
```

## Documentary Camera Language

### Handheld Observational

```
Camera: handheld, follows the subject
Movement: reactive — follows what happens, not anticipatory
Frame: sometimes imperfect — subject partially obscured, edge of frame
Zoom: natural use of zoom as events unfold and camera adjusts
```

### Interview Setup

```
Subject: [not looking at camera — looking at interviewer off-frame]
Framing: [medium / medium-CU], subject at rule-of-thirds
Background: [relevant to their story / deliberately ambiguous / location-specific]
Lighting: [interview light — key from one side, soft, fills the face honestly]
Audio: lapel mic — clean, intimate, close
```

### B-Roll / Cutaway

```
FUNCTION: [illustrate / extend / contradict / provide context for] what narrator/subject says
Visual: [specific to what is being discussed — not generic]
Duration: [held long enough to read, cut before it's exhausted]
Lighting: [available / lightly supplemented]
```

## Subject Framing

For documentary subjects (real people or played subjects):

```
CHARACTER: [Name], [role/context]
VISUAL TRUTH: documentary subjects look like real people — not idealized.
              Show age, fatigue, specific physical characteristics.
INTERVIEW FRAME: [medium to MCU], subject at [rule of thirds position],
                 looking toward off-camera interviewer
EMOTIONAL STATE: [what they are carrying in this moment of the interview]
BACKGROUND: [what does the environment say about who they are?]
```

## Nature / Wildlife Documentary

```
STYLE: nature documentary, David Attenborough school
Camera: [stabilized telephoto for wildlife / steady wide for landscape]
Lighting: [available — golden hour / midday / blue hour — the actual light of the place]
Creature: [specific species], [specific behaviour being documented]
Distance: [telephoto — creature unaware of camera / managed approach]
Audio: [environment sounds / creature sounds / no music during behaviour / 
        score for dramatic narration moments]
Narration: [calm authority, close observation, species-specific detail]
```

---

## Golden Rule

> A documentary camera is still a camera — every choice about where it points is a directorial choice. Own those choices. The truth is in what you chose to show and how you chose to frame it.
