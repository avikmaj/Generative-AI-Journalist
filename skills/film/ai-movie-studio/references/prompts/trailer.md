# Trailer Prompts — Cinematic Trailers & Teasers

## The Trailer Is Its Own Film Form

A trailer is not a collection of best moments from the film — it is a short film with its own structure, designed to create a desire to see the larger work. It has its own grammar.

---

## Trailer Structure Formula

```
ACT 1 — WORLD AND HOOK (0:00–0:20)
  Establish the world at rest.
  Introduce the protagonist.
  State the inciting incident in one image.

ACT 2 — ESCALATION (0:20–1:20)
  Rising stakes, intercutting between story elements.
  Character relationships glimpsed.
  The threat / conflict escalates through visual montage.
  Music building.
  
BEAT DROP / TURN (1:20–1:25)
  A moment of silence or music cut. The audience inhales.
  The highest-stakes image or line of dialogue.

ACT 3 — CHAOS / CLIMAX IMAGERY (1:25–2:00)
  Rapid cutting. Maximum energy.
  The film's most spectacular images.
  Music at maximum.
  
FINAL BEAT (2:00–2:20)
  Sudden silence or music reduction.
  One final image — a joke / an emotional gut-punch / a mystery.
  Title card hold.
```

## Trailer Scene Prompt Templates

### The World Reveal

```
OPENING: wide establishing shot of the film's world.
No characters yet. Let the world breathe.
Narration (if applicable): the first line that frames everything.
Music: establishing theme at low key, building.
Duration: hold longer than seems comfortable.
Visual quality: the most beautiful version of this world.
```

### The Protagonist Introduction

```
SHOT: [protagonist identity token], [doing something that tells us who they are].
Camera: begins on a detail, reveals the person.
No dialogue yet. We are reading them.
Music: [their theme, or silence].
Duration: 3–5 seconds — enough to register, not enough to fully know.
```

### The Escalation Montage

```
MONTAGE STRUCTURE: rapid intercutting, increasing pace.
SHOT TYPES: alternating between:
  - Wide world shots (scale)
  - Character reaction shots (stakes)
  - Action beats (momentum)
  - Environment establishing (world complexity)
AUDIO: music building underneath, key sound effects on impacts.
PACING: cut every 2–3 seconds at start, accelerating to 0.5–1 second cuts at peak.
```

### The Money Shot

```
THE SINGLE MOST SPECTACULAR IMAGE IN THE TRAILER.
This is not chosen for plot clarity — it is chosen for emotional impact.
COMPOSITION: maximum impact version of the film's visual thesis.
DURATION: hold slightly longer than the cuts around it — the eye needs to complete it.
AUDIO: a brief moment of silence before / music peak hits simultaneously / 
        the single most important line of dialogue.
```

### Title Card Hold

```
TITLE CARD: [FILM TITLE]
FONT: [specific to the film's visual identity]
BACKGROUND: [black / the film's dominant colour / final frame of the trailer]
AUDIO: silence, or music resolving to a single held note.
HOLD DURATION: 3 seconds minimum — let it register.
ADDITIONAL INFO: [release date / cast / tagline — one line maximum]
```

## Teaser Trailer (Under 90 Seconds)

The teaser creates desire without revealing content. It sells a feeling, not a story.

```
TEASER STRUCTURE:
  0:00–0:30: World without context. Mystery. Something is coming but we don't know what.
  0:30–0:50: A glimpse. One image of the protagonist. One image of the threat.
  0:50–1:00: Title card only.
  
TEASER PRINCIPLE: withhold more than you reveal. The audience should leave the 
                   teaser asking one specific question.
```

## Trailer Music Direction

```
TRAILER SCORE TYPE:
  Opening: [sparse, atmospheric — establish world tone]
  Building: [orchestral swell / hybrid electronic / licensed track beginning]
  Beat drop: [the moment where music style or tempo changes — the audience physically responds]
  Montage: [driving, percussive, tempo matching cut pace]
  Final: [resolution chord / silence / one last unexpected note]
```

---

## Golden Rule

> A great trailer makes the audience feel they have already experienced the film emotionally — without having seen it. Design the feeling, not the plot summary.
