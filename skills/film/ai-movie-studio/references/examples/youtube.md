# YouTube / Short-Form Video — Worked Example

## Example: "WHAT REMAINS" — 90-Second Dramatic Short

*Optimized for YouTube Shorts / vertical format, then repurposed to 16:9*

---

## FORMAT STRATEGY

```
PRIMARY FORMAT: 9:16 vertical (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok)
SECONDARY: 16:9 horizontal (YouTube main)
DURATION: 90 seconds
CLIPS: 4 clips of approximately 22 seconds each
STORY: Complete, self-contained. Beginning → conflict → resolution → final image.
```

---

## STORY BRIEF

```
TITLE: What Remains
PREMISE: A soldier finds a photograph in the ruins of a building. 
         He cannot know whose it was. He keeps it anyway.
THEME: The persistence of the particular in the midst of the universal.
TARGET EMOTION: The ache of what we cannot return to its owner.
RUNTIME: 90 seconds.
```

---

## SHORTS STRUCTURE (4 Clips)

### Clip 1 (0:00–0:22): SETUP

```
BEATS:
0–5s: Wide establishing — ruined building exterior. Aftermath. He searches.
5–15s: Interior — rubble. His hands moving through debris.
15–22s: He finds something. Stops.

EMOTIONAL TONE: the careful attention of someone in the middle of devastation.
GRADE: desaturated, grey, the colour of aftermath.
CAMERA: handheld medium following character, then CU on hands and object.
LIGHTING: daylight through broken structure, shafts of light in dust.
SFX: rubble shifting, wind through the ruin, distant sound of ongoing conflict.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: none — let the story carry itself.
```

### Clip 2 (0:22–0:44): DEVELOPMENT

```
BEATS:
22–30s: CU on the photograph — a family. We see it as he sees it.
30–40s: His face. He processes what it means.
40–44s: He looks around the destroyed space. The family was here.

EMOTIONAL TONE: recognition of the particular — not statistics but someone's life.
CAMERA: CU on photograph insert, then CU on face.
LIGHTING: shafts of daylight through dust.
LIP SYNC: none — his face carries everything.
```

### Clip 3 (0:44–1:06): COMPLICATION

```
BEATS:
44–56s: He moves to leave. He should put it down. He doesn't.
56–66s: He looks at the photograph again. A child's face.

EMOTIONAL TONE: the weight of a choice that is not really a choice.
CAMERA: medium OTS (his back), then MCU.
PERFORMANCE NOTE: the character trying to talk himself out of what he's about to do, 
                  then failing.
```

### Clip 4 (1:06–1:30): RESOLUTION + FINAL IMAGE

```
BEATS:
66–80s: He folds the photograph carefully. Places it in his chest pocket. 
        Against his heart, literally, though he'd never say so.
80–90s: He walks back out into the ruined street. The camera holds on him 
        receding into the aftermath until he's small.

EMOTIONAL TONE: the act of carrying what cannot be returned.
CAMERA: MCU on the placement of the photograph, then wide pull-back as he walks out.
FINAL IMAGE: His figure small in the ruined street, walking forward, carrying it.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: [title card held]: "What Remains"
AUDIO: score enters here for the first time — minimal, single instrument, holds.
```

## Scaling Up from Shorts

This 90-second structure is the prototype. The same story can scale:
- 3 minutes: expand each beat, add a scene between clips 2 and 3
- 7 minutes (short film format): full scene development, dialogue added
- Feature (setup): this is scene 12 in a larger war film

The brief, the characters, and the emotional target remain identical at any scale.

---

## Golden Rule

> A 90-second film has the same obligations as a 90-minute one: a beginning, a middle, an end, and something the audience carries with them after the screen goes dark.
