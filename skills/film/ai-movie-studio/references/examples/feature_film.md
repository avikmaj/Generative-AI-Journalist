# Feature Film — Complete Worked Example

## Example Project: "THE LAST HANDLER"
*A crime thriller*

---

## PRODUCTION BIBLE (Abbreviated)

```
TITLE: The Last Handler
GENRE: Crime Thriller
LOGLINE: A burned DEA handler must protect the informant who destroyed his career 
         when a cartel kill team tracks them across three countries.
THEME: Trust and betrayal — whether betrayal ever earns the right to trust again.
TARGET EMOTION: Earned relief shadowed by loss — the mission succeeded; 
                 something essential did not survive it.
TONE: Cold, precise, morally grey. No heroes. Choices with consequences.
VISUAL STYLE: Photorealistic. Teal-cold desaturated grade. 
              Practical light only. Anamorphic widescreen.
SCORE: Minimal. Pulse-bass tension underscore. No melodic comfort.
```

---

## CHARACTER LOCKS

```
IDENTITY TOKEN — COLE (Handler):
Male, mid-40s, lean and worn down — the years visible in the way he holds himself. 
Face angular, jaw set, dark circles that have been there long enough to be permanent. 
Grey beginning at the temples, short and unmaintained. Eyes dark brown, always 
measuring. Three-day stubble. Charcoal wool coat, open. Dark grey shirt. 
Black trousers, worn at the knee. Shoes: black lace-up, scuffed. 
No accessories. Posture: slightly forward, like someone leaning into wind 
they've been walking against for years.

IDENTITY TOKEN — VERA (Informant):
Female, early 30s, expensively put together but showing cracks. Face precise, 
high cheekbones, dark eyes that assess everything. Hair dark brown, pulled back 
practically. Tailored jacket, dark green, good material. Black trousers. 
Ankle boots, low heel. Small bag worn across the body. 
Posture: held perfectly — she has learned that perfect posture is armor.
```

## ACT 1 — SCENE PACKAGE

### Scene 1 — Opening (Cole Alone, Pre-Incident)

```
REF IMAGES: @cole_character_sheet + @environment_city_night

EMOTIONAL TONE: the quiet exhaustion of a man who has made his last mistake 
                and is living with the noise of it.

VISUAL STYLE: photorealistic, teal-blue cold grade, anamorphic 2.39:1

IDENTITY: COLE — [paste full token verbatim]

COMPOSITION: medium shot, Cole at left third, looking right toward dark city window.
             Negative space to his right. Low ceiling.

LIGHTING: interior night, single tungsten desk lamp, 2700K warm but 
          surrounded by cold blue ambient from window. Low fill.

CAMERA: 85mm, static, holds. The camera does not move. 
        We watch him watching nothing.

VFX: rain on the exterior window, city lights refracted.
SFX: rain on glass, distant traffic, HVAC hum.
DIALOGUE: none.
NARRATOR: none.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: "BUDAPEST — THREE YEARS EARLIER"

NEGATIVE: no extra light sources, no music atmosphere, no additional characters
```

### Scene 2 — The Assignment (Meeting with Control)

```
REF IMAGES: @cole_character_sheet + @control_character_sheet + @environment_federal_office

EMOTIONAL TONE: the bureaucratic delivery of a mission nobody wants to give 
                and nobody wants to accept.

VISUAL STYLE: photorealistic, institutional cold, fluorescent overhead

IDENTITY A: COLE — [paste full token]
IDENTITY B: CONTROL (male, 55, the comfortable danger of someone who gives 
            orders in nice suits — charcoal suit, white shirt, gold tie pin, 
            silver hair perfect)

COMPOSITION: two-shot across a table. Equal frame weight. Power balance.

LIGHTING: fluorescent overhead, cold 6500K, flat. No fill needed — 
          this light has no warmth to offer.

CAMERA: 50mm, static two-shot establishing, then OTS coverage.
        The camera respects the formality.

SFX: office ambient — air conditioning, distant corridor sound.
DIALOGUE: 
  CONTROL (even, professional, the threat below the surface):
  "She's asking for you specifically."
  COLE (flat, not surprised — nothing surprises him anymore):
  "She can ask for someone else."
ON-SCREEN TEXT: none.

NEGATIVE: no warm lighting, no comfortable framing
```

## ACT 2 — ACTION SEQUENCE EXAMPLE

### Scene 14 — Corridor Fight (Cole vs. Kill Team — Member 1)

```
REF IMAGES: @cole_character_sheet + @environment_hotel_corridor

EMOTIONAL TONE: survival — not heroism. The desperation of someone 
                fighting to stay alive, not to win.

VISUAL STYLE: photorealistic, desaturated, practical emergency lighting only

IDENTITY: COLE — [paste full token, note: collar torn, jacket missing by this point]

ENVIRONMENT: hotel corridor, emergency lighting only — red strips at floor level, 
             one overhead fixture broken and flickering. Carpet. Narrow. 
             No room to maneuver.

COMBAT NOTE: Cole is not a combat movie hero. He fights economically, 
             with damage — this is a man fighting with a plan running out.

COMPOSITION: tight medium-handheld, cuts on action.

LIGHTING: red emergency strip lighting from below, one flickering overhead, 
          practical door light at far end — 80% of frame in near-darkness.

CAMERA: handheld medium, reactive not anticipatory. 
        Tight to the action. Every cut has an action anchor.

ACTION BEAT:
ATTACKER comes from [room door left]. COLE reacts late — takes the first hit.
Stumbles into the wall. Assesses. 
He doesn't fight back immediately — he creates distance, 
buys time to think. Then: controlled, economic, the moves of someone 
who has done this before but hates doing it.
ATTACKER goes down. COLE does not celebrate. He checks the corridor. Breathes.

SFX: impact sounds (dull, physical), body against wall, clothing strain, 
     broken overhead light continuing to flicker, distant alarm.
MUSIC: none. The sounds of the fight ARE the music.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: none.

NEGATIVE: no action-hero posturing, no cartoonish impact effects, 
          no clean choreography — this is ugly and effective
```

## ACT 3 — CLIMAX EXAMPLE

### Scene 28 — The Warehouse (Final Confrontation)

```
REF IMAGES: @cole_character_sheet + @vera_character_sheet + @environment_warehouse

EMOTIONAL TONE: the price of everything. Nothing is free. Including survival.

VISUAL STYLE: photorealistic, coldest grade of the film.

ENVIRONMENT: warehouse, industrial port. Night. Rain outside audible. 
             Interior: overhead industrial sodium lamps, orange-amber pools 
             with deep black between. Vast space.

IDENTITY COLE: [paste token, note: injured — left arm, coat torn, face bloodied]
IDENTITY VERA: [paste token, note: hands bound, seated, present]

COMPOSITION SEQUENCE:
  Wide: the vast warehouse, Cole small in left frame, 
        antagonist at right, Vera between them at far end.
  Medium Cole: the decision being processed.
  OTS on antagonist: the offer being made.
  CU Cole: the answer.

LIGHTING: sodium industrial overhead, amber pools, 
          black between them, rain light at windows.

CAMERA: [see sequence above], final shot: 
        wide pull-back as the decision is executed, 
        Cole small against the scale of the warehouse and the cost.

SFX: rain on roof, industrial ambient, [specific sounds of the scene's action].
MUSIC: first time the score rises to full statement — 
       the hero's theme, inverted — same notes, minor, slower.
       Then: silence.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: none until aftermath.
```

## THE FINAL IMAGE

```
FINAL SHOT OF THE FILM:

EMOTIONAL TONE: the specific ache of winning and losing simultaneously.

COMPOSITION: exterior, dawn. Cole alone. 
             The city beginning behind him. His back is to camera.
             He does not turn. He walks toward the light.

CAMERA: wide, static, holds until he becomes small and disappears.

MUSIC: the hero's inverted theme at single piano — then silence before cut to black.

LIGHTING: blue hour transitioning to first gold. 
          The world waking up around someone it almost broke.

HOLD: longer than comfortable. Let the audience sit in it.
```

## Production Notes

- Total clips required: approximately 40–50 for a 90-minute feature
- Grade is consistent throughout: teal-cold, desaturated, never warm except when contrast is the point
- Score never exceeds what the silence earns
- The film earns its violence by showing the cost of it
