## Human Resources Management (Book 41)

**HR lifecycle:**
Workforce planning → Recruitment → Onboarding →
Development → Performance → Retention → Offboarding

**Strategic HR pillars:**
- Talent acquisition (right people, right role, right time)
- Learning & development (continuous capability building)
- Performance management (clear expectations + fair assessment)
- Compensation & benefits (competitive + equitable)
- Employee relations (culture, engagement, conflict)
- HR analytics (data-driven people decisions)

## Employee Engagement (Book 42)

**Engagement drivers (Gallup Q12 summary):**
1. I know what is expected of me
2. I have the resources to do my work
3. I do what I do best every day
4. I have received recognition in the past 7 days
5. My manager cares about me as a person
6. Someone encourages my development
7. My opinion counts
8. The mission makes me feel my work is important
9. My colleagues are committed to quality
10. I have a best friend at work
11. Someone has talked to me about my progress
12. I have had opportunities to grow

**Engagement → Performance link:**
Highly engaged teams = 21% more profitable (Gallup)
Disengaged employees cost 34% of their salary in lost productivity

## Employee Retention Blueprint (Book 43)

**Top reasons employees leave:**
1. Poor manager relationship
2. Lack of growth/advancement
3. Compensation misalignment
4. Poor culture fit / toxic environment
5. Work-life balance failure
6. Lack of recognition
7. Unclear role or poor onboarding

**Stay interview questions (proactive retention):**
- What makes you stay here?
- What would make you leave?
- What would make your work more fulfilling?
- What would you change about your role?

**Retention formula:** Fair pay + Good manager + Growth path + Belonging

## Performance Management (Book 44)

**Performance management cycle:**
Set goals → Ongoing check-ins → Mid-year review →
Year-end review → Calibration → Reward/development action

**9-Box Talent Grid:**
```
           Low Performance  Medium  High Performance
High Potential  |  Dilemma  | Growth | Star
Medium Potential| Detractor | Core   | High Professional
Low Potential   | Detractor | Solid  | Highly Valued
```

**OKR Framework (Objectives & Key Results):**
Objective: qualitative, inspirational, time-bound
Key Results: measurable, specific, 3–5 per objective, 70% stretch

**Performance conversation structure:**
1. Acknowledge what's going well (specific)
2. Discuss what needs to change (behavior, not person)
3. Agree on actions and timeline
4. Confirm support available
5. Schedule follow-up

## Team Harmony Blueprint (Book 45)

**Tuckman Model:**
Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning

**High-performing team characteristics:**
- Psychological safety (Edmondson / Google Project Aristotle)
- Clear roles and accountability
- Shared goals and metrics
- Constructive conflict (disagree and commit)
- Trust and mutual respect
- Regular feedback loops

**Team health indicators:**
- Meetings are productive and end with clear actions
- People speak up with concerns and ideas
- Conflict is addressed, not avoided
- Accountability is mutual, not just top-down

## 360-Degree Feedback (Book 46)

**360 feedback process:**
1. Define competencies to assess
2. Select raters (manager, peers, direct reports, self)
3. Collect ratings anonymously
4. Generate report (aggregate, not individual raters)
5. Facilitate debrief with coach or manager
6. Create development plan from insights
7. Follow up at 6–12 months

**360 golden rules:**
- Not for compensation decisions
- Rater anonymity is non-negotiable
- Results require coaching support
- Focus on 2–3 development areas max (not every gap)
