## The four shapes worth memorising

Most business worksheets are one of these. Reach for the shape, then adapt the labels.

**1 · Scored matrix** — options as rows, weighted criteria as columns, score and total. Use for
vendor choice, prioritisation, hiring, market selection. Always state the weights before scoring, or
the result rationalises a decision already made.

**2 · Two-by-two** — two axes, four quadrants, one action per quadrant. Use for risk (likelihood ×
impact), tasks (Eisenhower: urgent × important), portfolio (BCG: growth × share), talent (9-box
condensed). Its value is the action per quadrant, not the plot.

**3 · Funnel or stage table** — stages as rows, with a conversion rate, owner and exit criterion per
stage. Use for sales pipeline, hiring pipeline, customer journey, stage-gate. The exit criterion is
what makes it a process rather than a diagram.

**4 · Plan-on-a-page** — objective, three to five bets, one metric per bet, owner, date. Use for
strategy, quarterly plans, OKRs, project charters. If it does not fit on one page, the plan is a wish
list.

## MoSCoW prioritisation

| Band | Meaning | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Must | Release fails without it | Cap at ~60% of capacity |
| Should | Important, painful to omit | Has a workaround |
| Could | Desirable if capacity allows | First to be cut |
| Won't | Explicitly out of scope this cycle | Write it down — this is the band that prevents scope creep |

The `Won't` band is the one people skip and the reason scope returns.

## BANT qualification

**Budget** — is money allocated, and who controls it? **Authority** — who signs, and have you met
them? **Need** — what breaks if they do nothing? **Timeline** — what event forces a decision by a
date?

A deal missing Authority or Timeline is not a deal; it is a conversation. Record which of the four is
unknown rather than assuming.

## Worksheet design rules

- One decision per sheet. A worksheet answering two questions answers neither.
- Every field either feeds a calculation or a decision. Delete fields that only describe.
- Put the output at the top, not the bottom — the user should see the conclusion first on review.
- Include the review cadence on the sheet itself, or it becomes a one-off document.
