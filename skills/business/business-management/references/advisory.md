# Advisory Reference — Risk, Consulting & Founders
## Business Management Bible — Risk Management, Founder Frameworks, Consulting Management

For consulting engagement phases and the Pyramid Principle, see `leadership.md`. For decision
frameworks, see `strategy.md`. This file covers risk and early-stage advisory.

## Risk categories

Classify every risk before scoring it — the category determines who owns it.

| Category | Covers | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic | Wrong market, wrong bet, competitor moves, obsolescence | Board / founder |
| Compliance | Regulation, licensing, data protection, contracts | Legal / compliance |
| Operational | Process failure, key-person loss, supplier failure, quality | Operations lead |
| Financial | Liquidity, credit, currency, cost overrun, concentration | Finance lead |
| Reputational | Public failure, ethics breach, customer backlash | Communications / CEO |
| IT / cyber | Breach, outage, data loss, vendor lock-in | CTO / security |
| Supply chain | Single-source dependency, logistics, geopolitical | Procurement |
| Project | Scope, schedule, resource, integration | Project manager |

## Risk assessment

Score each risk on two axes, then act by quadrant:

```
Likelihood × Impact = Exposure
```

| Likelihood | Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | **Avoid or mitigate now** — treat as a live programme with an owner |
| Low | High | **Transfer or plan** — insure, contract out, write the contingency |
| High | Low | **Mitigate cheaply** — process fix, automation, checklist |
| Low | Low | **Accept and log** — review at cadence, do not spend on it |

The four responses are the only ones: **avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept**. "Monitor" is not a
response — it is what you do after choosing one.

## Risk monitoring and reporting

- Every risk has a named owner, a review date and a current status. An unowned risk is not managed.
- Report movement, not the list. What changed since last review, and what became live.
- Track **residual** risk after mitigation, not just inherent risk. Sign-off is a conscious
  acceptance of residual risk.
- Add a **pre-mortem** to any significant initiative: assume it failed, and write why. This surfaces
  risks the register misses because nobody wanted to name them.

## Founder frameworks — early stage

**From idea to action.** An idea is a hypothesis about a problem someone will pay to remove. Write the
problem before the product, and name who has it and what they do today instead.

**Validation sequence** — do these in order; skipping ahead is the common failure:

1. Problem interviews — confirm the problem exists and hurts. No pitching.
2. Willingness to pay — a price conversation, not a "would you use this".
3. Smallest testable offer — a manual or concierge version before any build.
4. Repeatability — can you get the second and third customer the same way as the first?
5. Only then, scale the build.

**Funding options, cheapest capital first:** revenue and pre-sales · bootstrapping · grants and
competitions · friends and family · angels · accelerators · venture capital · debt. Each step trades
control and time for money. Take the cheapest capital that clears the constraint, and be explicit
about what dilution buys.

**Founder-specific risks:** co-founder misalignment without a written agreement · building before
validating · hiring ahead of revenue · a single-channel acquisition dependency · founder as the only
salesperson · no cash runway visibility.

## Advisory output discipline

Lead with the answer, then support it. Name the decision the client actually faces, state the
recommendation, then give the reasoning and the trade-off. Never present analysis with the
recommendation buried at the end.

When the client's framing is wrong, say so first — problem definition is the phase most often skipped
and most often fatal.
