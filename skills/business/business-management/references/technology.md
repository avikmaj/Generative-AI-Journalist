## Artificial Intelligence in Business (Book 47)

**AI application categories:**
- Automation: repetitive tasks, rule-based decisions
- Augmentation: human + AI working together
- Prediction: ML models forecasting outcomes
- Generation: content, code, design (GenAI)

**AI adoption framework:**
1. Identify high-volume, repetitive processes
2. Assess data availability and quality
3. Build/buy/partner decision
4. Pilot with measurable outcomes
5. Scale with governance and monitoring

**AI governance essentials:**
- Explainability: can you explain the AI decision?
- Fairness: does it discriminate against protected groups?
- Accountability: who owns AI outputs?
- Data privacy: GDPR/CCPA compliance

---

## Machine Learning (Book 48)

**ML categories:**
- Supervised learning: labeled data → predictions
- Unsupervised learning: find patterns in unlabeled data
- Reinforcement learning: agent learns via reward/penalty

**Business ML use cases:**
Customer churn prediction · Demand forecasting · Fraud detection ·
Recommendation engines · Credit scoring · Sentiment analysis ·
Predictive maintenance · Price optimization

**ML project success factors:**
- Problem correctly framed (ML is not always the right tool)
- Data quality >> model sophistication
- Baseline established before ML deployed
- Monitoring post-deployment (model drift)

## Cyber Security (Book 49)

**CIA Triad:**
Confidentiality · Integrity · Availability

**Security threat types:**
- Phishing: social engineering via email/message
- Ransomware: encrypt data, demand payment
- DDoS: overwhelm systems with traffic
- Insider threat: malicious or negligent employee
- Supply chain attack: compromise via vendor

**Business security priorities:**
1. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all accounts
2. Regular backups (tested, offline copy)
3. Employee security training (phishing simulation)
4. Patch management (vulnerabilities exploited within days)
5. Incident response plan (before you need it)

**Cost of breach:** Average $4.45M per breach (IBM 2023 report cited in Bible)

# Advisory Reference
## Risk, Consulting, Founder Frameworks — Books 8, 15, 16

**Consulting engagement red flags:**
- Client defines solution before problem is diagnosed
- No executive sponsor with real authority
- Success metrics not agreed upfront
- Scope not documented and signed

**Risk management maturity levels:**
1. Ad hoc: risks managed reactively
2. Repeatable: basic risk register exists
3. Defined: risk process is documented
4. Managed: risks quantified and tracked
5. Optimizing: risk integrated into all decisions

**Founder decision filters:**
- Does this extend runway?
- Does this get us closer to product-market fit?
- Does this scale beyond me?
- Would I regret not doing this in 10 years?
