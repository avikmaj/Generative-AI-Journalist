## Project Management (Book 17)

**Project lifecycle:**
Initiation → Planning → Execution → Monitoring & Control → Closure

**PMBOK Knowledge Areas:**
Integration · Scope · Schedule · Cost · Quality ·
Resource · Communications · Risk · Procurement · Stakeholders

**Project charter must contain:**
- Objectives and success criteria
- Scope (in/out)
- Key stakeholders
- Milestones
- Budget envelope
- Risks and assumptions
- Sponsor sign-off

**Triple constraint:** Scope ↔ Time ↔ Cost (quality is the outcome)
Change one → must adjust at least one other.

## Project Execution Blueprint (Book 18)

**Execution failure modes (most common):**
1. Unclear ownership (RACI not defined)
2. Scope creep without budget adjustment
3. Stakeholder misalignment discovered late
4. Risk mitigation planned but never executed
5. No regular cadence of review and accountability

**RACI Matrix:**
R = Responsible (does the work)
A = Accountable (owns the outcome — one person only)
C = Consulted (input before decision)
I = Informed (told after decision)

**Weekly execution rhythm:**
Monday: priorities confirmed ·
Daily: 15-min standup ·
Friday: wins/blockers/next week planned

## Agile (Book 19)

**Agile Manifesto — 4 values:**
1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
4. Responding to change over following a plan

**12 Agile Principles (summary):**
- Deliver working product frequently (weeks not months)
- Welcome changing requirements, even late
- Business and developers work together daily
- Build around motivated individuals
- Face-to-face conversation is most efficient
- Working product is the primary measure of progress
- Sustainable development pace
- Technical excellence enhances agility
- Simplicity — maximize work not done
- Best architectures emerge from self-organizing teams
- Regular reflection and adaptation

## Scrum (Book 20)

**Scrum roles:**
- Product Owner: owns backlog, prioritizes value
- Scrum Master: removes impediments, facilitates
- Development Team: self-organizing, cross-functional

**Scrum ceremonies:**
| Ceremony | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint Planning | Start of sprint | Select backlog items for sprint |
| Daily Scrum | Daily | Sync, surface blockers |
| Sprint Review | End of sprint | Demonstrate working increment |
| Sprint Retrospective | End of sprint | Inspect and adapt the process |

**Sprint:** 1–4 weeks fixed time-box. Never extend.
**Definition of Done:** explicit agreement on "complete."

## Kanban (Book 21)

**Kanban core principles:**
1. Visualize the workflow
2. Limit Work in Progress (WIP)
3. Manage flow
4. Make process policies explicit
5. Implement feedback loops
6. Improve collaboratively

**WIP limits:** The #1 Kanban practice.
More WIP = slower flow. Less WIP = faster delivery.

**Kanban metrics:**
- Lead time: request → delivery
- Cycle time: work started → delivery
- Throughput: items completed per period
- CFD (Cumulative Flow Diagram): visualize bottlenecks

## Process Improvement (Books 22–23)

**PDCA Cycle:** Plan → Do → Check → Act (repeat)

**Six Sigma DMAIC:**
Define → Measure → Analyze → Improve → Control

**Lean principles:**
1. Define value (from customer perspective)
2. Map the value stream
3. Create flow (eliminate waste)
4. Establish pull (not push)
5. Pursue perfection

**8 Wastes (Lean — TIMWOODS):**
Transport · Inventory · Motion · Waiting ·
Overproduction · Over-processing · Defects · Skills (unused)

**Process mapping:** Always map current state before designing future state.
Swim-lane diagrams for cross-functional clarity.

## Productivity & KPIs (Books 24–25)

**KPI design principles:**
- Tied to strategic objective
- Measurable with available data
- Actionable (someone can influence it)
- Time-bound
- Balanced (leading and lagging indicators)

**KPI hierarchy:**
Company KPIs → Department KPIs → Individual KPIs
(cascade ensures alignment)

**Productivity frameworks:**
- Time blocking: protect deep work hours
- Eat the frog: hardest task first
- Parkinson's Law: work expands to fill time allotted — set constraints
- 80/20: 20% of activities produce 80% of results — identify and protect them

**KPI Mastery — common mistakes:**
- Too many KPIs (max 5 per level)
- Measuring activity not outcome
- No review cadence
- KPIs not linked to incentives
